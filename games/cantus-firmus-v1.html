      How to enter and edit

      Press the Edit button to place and change notes; with it off, a tap or click on the score only selects. Pick a note value, then left-click on the staff where the note should go: between two notes inserts it there, after the last note adds it at the end. A left-click on a rest puts a note in its place. Right-click a note to turn it into a rest of the same value; right-click a rest to remove it. Left-click a note to select it (Play and the space bar then start from it); drag it up or down to change its pitch. Click the clef to change it, or the faint B flat beside it to add one.

      With a note selected, the value, rest and accidental buttons change that note. With nothing selected, they apply to the next note you enter. On a touch screen, tap a note to select it. Tap an empty place on the staff to put a note down there; the Undo, Redo and Delete buttons are at the top. Press and hold a note to turn it into a rest, or to remove a rest. Scrolling the page with a finger on the score does nothing to it.

      In tempus perfectum (click the C on the staff to turn it into O) the notes are read as the theorists teach: a brevis lasts three semibreves, a single semibreve next to a brevis makes it imperfect, and of two semibreves between breves the second is doubled. A dot after a brevis keeps it perfect; a dot between two semibreves separates the perfections; a dot after a semibreve followed by a minima adds half its value. In tempus imperfectum every dot adds half the value.

      Sing turns on the microphone. The note you sing shows as a faint red dot on the staff: next to the note being played, or before the final bar when nothing plays. Hover over Sing, or tap its arrow, for its switches. Flexible pitch: on, the dot follows your exact pitch and sits a little above or below its line or space when you are sharp or flat; off, it keeps to the nearest note, like a written second voice. Headphones: turn it on when you wear them, so unisons and octaves with the organ show too. With it off, the organ is filtered out of what the microphone hears, and a note sung in unison with the organ or an octave above it does not show. Notate: while the cantus firmus plays, what you sing is written out in red on a staff of its own, above the cantus firmus or below it when you sing below. Long notes are written on the strong beats even when you come in a little early or late; quick notes can be as short as fusae. Diatonic: the notes written are only those of the scale (with B or B flat as the cantus firmus has it), so a note sung out of tune is not written as a sharp or flat. It stays until you play again or change the cantus firmus. This one is an experiment: with headphones on it gets the most right. Latency, in the same menu, is the delay between the organ and your voice reaching the microphone: left empty, it is worked out from where your notes fall; Clap test measures it (clap with each of eight clicks); or type it in milliseconds.
